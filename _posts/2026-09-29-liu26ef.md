  Strategic Adaptation in Repeated Negotiation'

abstract: 'While large language models (LLMs) have emerged as powerful decision-makers
  across a wide range of single-agent and stationary environments, fewer efforts have
  been devoted to settings where LLMs must engage in <em>repeated</em> and <em>strategic</em>
  interactions with unknown or dynamic opponents. In such settings, recipes built
  upon <em>offline</em> pre-training or fine-tuning, though robust against worst-case
  adversaries, do not fully exploit the capability of LLMs to adapt <em>online</em>
  based on interaction feedback. Instead, we explore the more natural perspective
  of <em>scaling inference-time computation</em> as a mechanism for adaptation, embedding
  the principles of a classical game-theoretical learning dynamic, <em>smooth Fictitious
  Play (sFP)</em>, into LLM inference: (i) for belief formation, we employ an auxiliary
  opponent model that in-context learns to imitate the <em>time-averaged</em> behavior
  of the opponent; (ii) for best response, we advance best-of-$N$ (BoN) sampling by
  simulating against the opponent model. Empirical evaluations on two distinct forms
  of repeated negotiation games demonstrate that our method enables significant performance
  improvement over online interaction compared to various baselines, offering a scalable
  and principled approach to strategic decision-making without any parameter updates.'
