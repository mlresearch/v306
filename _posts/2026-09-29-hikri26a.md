title: Ambiguous Strategic Classification
openreview: TUixKAjYue
abstract: A common assumption in strategic classification is that the classifier is
  public knowledge. However, it remains unclear whether, and why, a system would choose
  to commit to full disclosure. We study a setting in which regulation requires the
  system to disclose some, but not all, of the information. This induces a learning
  task in which the learner must jointly optimize the classifier and the uncertainty
  surrounding it. To this end, we adopt from robust mechanism design the notion of
  <em>ambiguity</em>, which in our setting allows the learner to reveal a set or range
  of possible classifiers, while privately choosing which of them to ultimately realize.
  We investigate how ambiguity affects the learning task, develop efficient algorithms
  for computing best-responses and training, and empirically explore strategic learning
  and its outcomes in this novel setting and using our approach.
