title: 'HyPER: Bridging Exploration and Exploitation for Scalable LLM Reasoning with
  Hypothesis Path Expansion and Reduction'
openreview: G29kBVeIZt
abstract: 'Scaling test-time compute with multi-path chain-of-thought can improve
  reasoning accuracy, but its gains hinge on an effective exploration–exploitation
  trade-off. Existing methods handle this trade-off in rigid ways: tree-structured
  search hard-codes exploration via brittle expansion rules that disrupt post-trained
  reasoning, while parallel reasoning over-explores redundant hypothesis paths and
  relies on a weak answer selection strategy. Driven by the insight that the optimal
  balance is <em>phase-dependent</em> and that correct vs. incorrect paths often <em>diverge
  only at late stages</em>, we reconceptualize test-time scaling as a dynamic <em>expand–reduce</em>
  control problem over a pool of hypothesis paths. We introduce <b>HyPER</b>, a <em>training-free
  online control policy</em> for MoE multi-path decoding that reallocates compute
  under a fixed budget using lightweight path statistics. HyPER features (i) an <em>online
  controller</em> that shifts from exploration to exploitation as the hypothesis pool
  evolves, (ii) an MoE-based token-level refinement primitive for efficient <em>generation-time
  exploitation</em> without full-path resampling, and (iii) a length- and confidence-aware
  aggregation rule to bridge the existence–selection gap for reliable <em>answer-time
  exploitation</em>. Extensive experimental results across four MoE models and diverse
  benchmarks demonstrate HyPER consistently achieves the accuracy–compute Pareto frontier,
  outperforming prior-art methods by 8-10% while reducing token consumption by 25-40%.'
