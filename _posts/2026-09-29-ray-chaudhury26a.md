title: Equilibrium Pricing in Oligopolistic Data Markets
openreview: TAqejOfEAJ
abstract: 'We study equilibrium pricing in oligopolistic data markets with budget-constrained
  buyers (e.g., ML companies purchasing data to improve model accuracy) and strategic
  data sellers. Sellers compete by setting prices for their datasets, giving rise
  to a pricing game whose pure Nash equilibria correspond to equilibrium prices. While
  equilibrium prices are guaranteed for rivalrous goods via competitive equilibrium,
  we show that the non-rivalry of data fundamentally alters this picture: an exact
  Nash equilibrium need not exist, and in fact no 1.364-approximate equilibrium exists
  under uniform pricing. We therefore investigate relaxed equilibrium notions. Allowing
  sellers to use beyond-uniform pricing—specifically, piecewise-linear convex pricing
  functions—guarantees approximate stability within a constant factor: there exists
  a pricing profile in which no seller can improve revenue by a factor of two by deviating
  to any uniform price (a 2-approximate Nash equilibrium). Finally, our simulations
  demonstrate fast convergence and empirical approximation guarantees that outperform
  the worst-case bound of 2.'
