title: 'FairMerging: Rethinking Model Merging through the Lens of Fairness'

abstract: "<em>Model merging</em> offers an appealing route to multi-task learning
  by composing independently fine-tuned checkpoints without centralized data or retraining.
  However, this convenience can come with a hidden cost. Model merging may <em>amplify</em>
  performance disparities across subgroups, raising fairness concerns even when average
  accuracy remains competitive. To explain this phenomenon, we develop a sensitivity-based
  theoretical analysis that upper bounds the fairness gap induced by model merging.
  Theoretical analysis with empirical verifications reveals that the resulting fairness
  gap is governed by two coupled factors, a <em>merging magnitude</em> term that measures
  how far the merged parameters move from the target model and <em>global sensitivity</em>
  terms that determine how unevenly the perturbation affects subgroup losses. Guided
  by these insights, we propose <em>FairMerging</em>, a two-stage merging framework
  that first reduces the sensitivity of the target model and then performs fairness-aware
  coefficient optimization with orthogonally normalized task vectors. Experiments
  across multiple datasets, backbones, and merging baselines demonstrate that FairMerging
  substantially mitigates unfairness while retaining competitive multi-task performance."
