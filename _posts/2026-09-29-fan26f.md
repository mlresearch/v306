abstract: 'Recent progress in large-scale robotic datasets and vision-language models
  (VLMs) has advanced research on vision-language-action (VLA) models. However, existing
  VLA models still face two fundamental challenges: (<em>i</em>) producing precise
  low-level actions from high-dimensional observations, (<em>ii</em>) bridging domain
  gaps across heterogeneous data sources, including diverse robot embodiments and
  human demonstrations. Existing methods often encode latent variables from either
  visual dynamics or robotic actions to guide policy learning, but they fail to fully
  exploit the complementary multi-modal knowledge present in large-scale, heterogeneous
  datasets. In this work, we present <b>XR-1</b>, a novel framework for versatile
  and scalable VLA learning across diverse robots, tasks, and environments. At its
  core, XR-1 introduces the <em>Unified Vision-Motion Codes (UVMC)</em>, a discrete
  latent representation learned via a dual-branch VQ-VAE that jointly encodes visual
  dynamics and robotic motion. UVMC addresses these challenges by (<em>i</em>) serving
  as an intermediate representation between the observations and actions, and (<em>ii</em>)
  aligning multimodal dynamic information from heterogeneous data sources to capture
  complementary knowledge. To effectively exploit UVMC, we propose a <em>three-stage
  training paradigm</em>: (<em>i</em>) self-supervised UVMC learning, (<em>ii</em>)
  UVMC-guided pretraining on large-scale cross-embodiment robotic datasets, and (<em>iii</em>)
  task-specific post-training. We validate XR-1 through extensive real-world experiments
  with more than 12,000 rollouts on six different robot embodiments, spanning over
  120 diverse manipulation tasks. XR-1 consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines
  such as $\pi_0$ and GR00T-N1.5 while demonstrating strong generalization to novel
  objects, background variations, distractors, and illumination changes. Our project
  is at https://xr-1-vla.github.io/.'
