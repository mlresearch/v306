abstract: 'Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as the de facto standard for privacy-preserving
  machine learning, largely due to Secure Aggregation (SecAgg) protocols rendering
  individual user contributions cryptographically invisible to the server; we contend
  that this pursuit of unconditional blind aggregation has produced a systemic vulnerability,
  the Privacy-Auditability Paradox. By making individual updates indistinguishable
  in the aggregate, default SecAgg creates a <em>Sanitization Gap</em> in which input-level
  poisoning is undetectable, and a <em>Regulatory Dead Zone</em> in which compliance
  with the EU AI Act’s robustness and traceability mandates are structurally undermined.
  In this position paper, we argue that the community must transition from <em>blind
  secure aggregation</em> to <b>Controllable Secure Aggregation (CSA)</b>, in which
  privacy is not a binary state of secrecy but a programmable spectrum of cryptographic
  access. CSA replaces binary secrecy with fine-grained, policy-based governance built
  on a complementary stack of functional encryption, zero-knowledge proofs, and threshold
  cryptography, yielding <em>Verified Blindness</em>: the server remains blind by
  default but admits a consensus-bound, cryptographically regulated break-glass channel
  for auditing specific inputs. Adopting CSA is not merely a technical upgrade but
  a necessary condition for transforming FL from an unregulated academic prototype
  into a robust, compliant, and trustworthy privacy infrastructure.'
