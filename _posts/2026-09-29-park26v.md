title: Bridging the Knowledge-Prediction Gap in LLMs on Multiple-Choice Questions
openreview: I0RgZXTO53
abstract: While large language models (LLMs) perform strongly on diverse tasks, their
  trustworthiness is limited by erratic behavior that is unfaithful to their internal
  knowledge. In particular, LLMs often fail on multiple-choice questions (MCQs) even
  if they encode correct answers in their hidden representations, revealing a misalignment
  between internal knowledge and output behavior. We investigate and mitigate this
  <em>knowledge-prediction gap</em> on MCQs through a three-step analysis of hidden
  representations. First, we quantify the prevalence and magnitude of the gap across
  models and datasets. Second, we provide a geometric interpretation by identifying
  distinct <em>knowledge</em> and <em>prediction</em> subspaces in the residual stream.
  Third, we introduce <b>KAPPA</b>, a lightweight inference-time intervention that
  aligns the two subspaces within the residual stream to reduce the knowledge-prediction
  gap. Our results provide a geometric and interpretable explanation of the knowledge-prediction
  gap in LLMs. Furthermore, KAPPA effectively reduces the gap across diverse MCQ benchmarks
  and models, and generalizes to free-form settings.
