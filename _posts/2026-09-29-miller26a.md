title: 'SEMIR: Semantic Minor-Induced Representation Learning on Graphs for Visual
  Segmentation'

abstract: 'Segmenting small and sparse structures in large-scale images is fundamentally
  constrained by voxel-level, lattice-bound computation and extreme class imbalance–dense,
  full-resolution inference scales poorly and forces most pipelines to rely on fixed
  regionization or downsampling, coupling computational cost to image resolution and
  attenuating boundary evidence precisely where minority structures are most informative.
  We introduce <b>SEMIR</b> (<em>Semantic Minor-Induced Representation Learning</em>),
  a representation framework that decouples inference from the native grid by learning
  a task-adapted, topology-preserving latent graph representation with exact decoding.
  <b>SEMIR</b> transforms the underlying grid graph into a compact, boundary-aligned
  graph minor through parameterized edge contraction, node deletion, and edge deletion,
  while preserving an exact lifting map from minor predictions to lattice labels.
  Minor construction is formalized as a few-shot structure learning problem that replaces
  hand-tuned preprocessing with a <em>boundary-alignment objective</em>: minor parameters
  are learned by maximizing agreement between predicted boundary elements and target-specific
  semantic edges under a <em>boundary Dice criterion</em>, and the induced minor is
  annotated with scale- and rotation-robust geometric and intensity descriptors and
  supports efficient region-level inference via message passing on a graph neural
  network (GNN) with relational edge features. We benchmark <b>SEMIR</b> on three
  tumor segmentation datasets—<b>BraTS 2021</b>, <b>KiTS23</b>, and <b>LiTS</b>—where
  targets exhibit high structural variability and distributional uncertainty. <b>SEMIR</b>
  yields consistent improvements in <em>minority-structure Dice</em> at practical
  runtime. More broadly, SEMIR establishes a framework for learning task-adapted,
  topology-preserving latent representations with exact decoding for high-resolution
  structured visual data.'
