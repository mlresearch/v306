abstract: 'Reasoning post-training with GRPO is typically built on <em>static uniformity</em>:
  uniform prompt sampling and a fixed number of rollouts per prompt. For heterogeneous,
  heavy-tailed reasoning data, this wastes compute on already-solved patterns while
  under-training the long tail of hard problems. We cast GRPO post-training as two
  independent GDRO games (not coupled) over <em>dynamic difficulty groups</em> defined
  by an <em>online train-time pass@$n$</em> statistic (computed from the rollouts
  used for GRPO updates): a <em>data adversary</em> that reshapes prompt sampling
  and a <em>compute adversary</em> that redistributes rollouts. <b>Prompt-GDRO</b>
  applies multiplicative-weights reweighting over bins (with an EMA-debiased difficulty
  score) to upweight persistently hard groups without frequency bias. <b>Rollout-GDRO</b>
  allocates rollouts across bins under a fixed mean budget via a shadow-price controller,
  improving gradient information efficiency on high-uncertainty groups while preserving
  the mean train-time rollout budget. Our approach is principled and theory-driven:
  we provide no-regret guarantees for the Prompt-GDRO game (via an entropy-regularized
  GDRO surrogate) and a variance-proxy analysis that yields a square-root optimal
  compute allocation for Rollout-GDRO. On DAPO 14.1k with Qwen3-Base (1.7B/4B/8B),
  each controller improves pass@8 by 9–13% over GRPO; targeted Qwen3-4B reruns reproduce
  the Prompt-GDRO gap under a second seed, show second-seed Rollout-GDRO gains on
  four of five comparable benchmarks, and show low sensitivity to several key hyperparameters.'
