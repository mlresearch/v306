abstract: We propose Bayesian Forest Thompson Sampling (BFTS), which performs Thompson
  sampling using arm-wise Bayesian Additive Regression Trees (BART) to model each
  action’s mean reward and generate MCMC-based posterior draws for decision-making.
  We derive an information-theoretic Bayesian regret bound of order $\widetilde{\mathcal
  O}(K\sqrt{T})$ for ideal posterior sampling under a correctly specified Bayesian
  design. Empirically, BFTS achieves competitive regret on nonlinear synthetic benchmarks
  with near-nominal uncertainty calibration, attains the best average rank across
  nine OpenML contextual bandit benchmarks, and yields higher estimated policy values
  than linear, neural, and tree-ensemble baselines in a Drink Less micro-randomized
  trial case study. Across OpenML benchmarks, BFTS is robust to hyperparameter choices.
