abstract: 'This position paper argues that <em>documentation is infrastructure</em>
  for reproducible geometry reasoning, and more broadly that mathematical benchmarks
  built around formal domain-specific languages (DSLs) need an executable interface
  contract: documented vocabulary should be matched by executable, versioned behavior
  and runnable examples. We use JGEX (as implemented by Newclid and AlphaGeometry)
  as a prototypical case study of how documentation–implementation gaps, missing examples,
  and nontrivial encoding choices can constrain expressivity, affect solver behavior,
  fragment tool interoperability, and bias benchmark construction. We introduce <em>JGEXDiv</em>,
  a curated collection of $137$ Euclidean geometry problems with released JGEX formalizations,
  natural-language rewrites, and metadata, covering $51.4%$ of admissible construction-pattern
  bins under our signature-based analysis. We also release a conformance suite for
  the documented JGEX interface, including minimal runnable examples, validation scripts,
  and a predicate-level support matrix. This audit surfaces cross-tool discrepancies,
  including documented predicates that execute under pinned Newclid v3.0.1 but are
  not recognized by Yuclid, as well as predicates that are unsupported or unstable
  under Newclid itself. Finally, we provide an executable NL$\rightarrow$JGEX baseline
  under the same backend. We argue that these artifacts together form an executable
  interface contract for mathematical DSL benchmarks, and should be treated as first-class
  research artifacts alongside datasets and evaluation code.'
