title: Prompt Injection as Role Confusion

abstract: 'LLMs see the world as a single stream of text, partitioned into <em>roles</em>
  like  or . We trace <b>prompt injection</b> to <b>role confusion</b>: models perceive
  the source of text from <em>how it sounds</em>, not its labeled role. A command
  hidden in a webpage hijacks an agent simply because it sounds like  text, despite
  its  label. We design <em>role probes</em> to measure how LLMs internally perceive
  "who is speaking", and find that injected text occupies the same representational
  space as the trusted role it imitates. We demonstrate this with CoT Forgery, a zero-shot
  attack that injects fabricated reasoning into user prompts and tool outputs. Models
  mistake the forgery for their own thoughts, yielding 60% attack success against
  frontier models with near-zero baselines. Strikingly, the degree of role confusion
  predicts attack success before a single token is generated. This mechanism generalizes
  beyond CoT Forgery to standard agent prompt injections, revealing prompt injection
  as a measurable consequence of role perception. To the model, sounding like a role
  is indistinguishable from being one.'
