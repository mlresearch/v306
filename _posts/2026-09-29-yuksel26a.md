title: Incremental Learning of Sparse Attention Patterns in Transformers
openreview: vSRh1qU5sH
abstract: Transformers integrate information from multiple past positions through
  sparse attention patterns that copy from specific locations. We study how these
  patterns form during training in a high-order Markov-chain setting, where the next
  token depends on several groups of past positions with differing statistical importance.
  Learning proceeds in discrete stages, each marked by the emergence of a sparse attention
  pattern for the next most important group. The dynamics are initially competitive,
  with all attention heads focusing on the dominant pattern, but later become cooperative,
  with different heads specializing in distinct patterns. For a tractable surrogate,
  the dynamics reduce to those of a tensor factorization problem, enabling us to prove
  stagewise convergence. Empirically, the number of stages completed depends on training
  set size, suggesting a form of implicit complexity control through early stopping.
  Overall, we give a theoretical account of how structured attention patterns and
  head specialization emerge without an explicit curriculum.
