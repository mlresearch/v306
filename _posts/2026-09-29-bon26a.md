abstract: We propose a novel framework for measuring privacy from a Bayesian game-theoretic
  perspective. This framework enables the creation of new, purpose-driven privacy
  definitions that are rigorously justified, while also allowing for the assessment
  of existing privacy guarantees through game theory. We show that pure and probabilistic
  differential privacy are special cases of our framework, and provide new interpretations
  of the post-processing inequality in these settings. Further, we demonstrate that
  privacy guarantees can be established for deterministic algorithms, which are overlooked
  by current privacy standards.
