abstract: The default paradigm of post-training text-to-image generators includes
  post-hoc selection of generated images, and subsequent training with one reward
  model to align the generator to the reward, typically user preference. This discards
  informative data as well as optimizes only for a single reward, hence harming diversity,
  semantic fidelity and efficiency. Instead, we propose MIRO, a method that conditions
  the model on multiple rewards during training, thus letting the model learn user
  preferences directly. MIRO pre-training both improves the visual quality of the
  generated images and speeds up the training, achieving state of the art on the GenEval
  compositional benchmark and user-preference scores (PickAScore, ImageReward, HPSv2).
