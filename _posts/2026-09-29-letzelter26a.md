abstract: 'We propose LoRA-MCL, a training scheme that extends next-token prediction
  in language models with a method designed to decode diverse, plausible sentence
  continuations at inference time. Traditional language modeling is an intrinsically
  ill-posed problem: given a context, multiple “futures” may be equally plausible.
  Our approach leverages Multiple Choice Learning (MCL) and the winner-takes-all loss
  to efficiently handle ambiguity through Low-Rank Adaptation. We provide a theoretical
  interpretation of applying MCL to language modeling, assuming the data is generated
  from a mixture of distributions. We illustrate the proposed approach using mixtures
  of Markov chains. We then demonstrate with experiments on audio and visual captioning,
  as well as machine translation, that our method achieves high diversity and relevance
  in generated outputs. We release the code for applying LoRA-MCL to a wide range
  of language models.'
