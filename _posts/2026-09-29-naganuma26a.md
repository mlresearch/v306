title: Adaptive Batch Sizes Using Non-Euclidean Gradient Noise Scales for Stochastic
  Sign and Spectral Descent
openreview: XMSaWRpEPS
abstract: To maximize hardware utilization, modern machine learning systems typically
  employ large constant or manually tuned batch size schedules, relying on heuristics
  that are brittle and costly to tune. Existing adaptive strategies based on gradient
  noise scale (GNS) offer a principled alternative. However, their assumption of SGD’s
  Euclidean geometry creates a fundamental mismatch with popular optimizers based
  on generalized norms, such as signSGD / Signum ($\ell_\infty$) and stochastic spectral
  descent (specSGD) / Muon (Schatten-$\infty$). In this work, we derive gradient noise
  scales for signSGD and specSGD that naturally emerge from the geometry of their
  respective dual norms. To practically estimate these non-Euclidean metrics, we propose
  an efficient variance estimation procedure that leverages the local mini-batch gradients
  on different ranks in distributed data-parallel systems. Our experiments demonstrate
  that adaptive batch size strategies using non-Euclidean GNS enable us to match the
  validation loss of constant-batch baselines while reducing training steps by up
  to 66% for Signum and Muon on a 160 million parameter Llama model.
