title: 'Ranking Free RAG: Replacing Re-ranking with Selection in RAG for Sensitive
  Domains'
openreview: O88FCPAPAj
abstract: 'Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems deployed in sensitive domains
  must provide interpretable evidence selection and robust safeguards against data
  poisoning, yet current approaches rely on opaque similarity-based retrieval with
  arbitrary top-k cutoffs that offer no explanation for their selections and remain
  vulnerable to adversarial manipulation. We propose METEORA, a rationale-driven RAG
  framework that addresses these fundamental limitations through interpretable, adaptive
  evidence retrieval. Our framework introduces three synergistic contributions. First,
  we preference-tune a general-purpose LLM to generate explicit rationales that articulate
  why specific evidence is needed for a given query. These rationales then guide adaptive
  evidence selection through a two-step process: rationale-chunk pairing for query-specific
  relevance assessment, followed by dynamic cutoff detection that eliminates the need
  for arbitrary k heuristics. Finally, the same rationales enable a verification stage
  that filters poisoned or misleading evidence before generation. Evaluation across
  six datasets demonstrates substantial improvements on three critical dimensions.
  For retrieval quality, METEORA achieves <b>21.05%</b> higher precision than the
  best-performing baseline, while its variant with context expansion achieves <b>13.41%</b>
  higher recall. In terms of efficiency, the framework reduces the volume of evidence
  required to reach comparable recall by <b>80%</b>, which directly translates to
  a <b>33.34%</b> improvement in downstream answer generation accuracy. Most notably
  for adversarial robustness, METEORA increases the F1 score from <b>0.10 to 0.44</b>
  under poisoning attacks, a 4.4$\times$ improvement that makes RAG systems substantially
  more resilient to adversarial manipulation. Human evaluation with four experienced
  annotators confirms genuine interpretability, achieving a mean confidence score
  of <b>3.64/5</b> and demonstrating that humans can reliably reconstruct evidence-level
  decisions with <b>86% accuracy</b>. These results demonstrate that rationale-driven
  retrieval can simultaneously enhance interpretability, efficiency, and safety in
  RAG systems for sensitive domains. The code is available in the anonymous GitHub
  repository https://anonymous.4open.science/r/METEORA-DC46/README.md'
