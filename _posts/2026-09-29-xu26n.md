abstract: Multimodal understanding of advertising videos is essential for interpreting
  the intricate relationship between visual storytelling and abstract persuasion strategies.
  However, despite excelling at general search, existing agents often struggle to
  bridge the cognitive gap between pixel-level perception and high-level marketing
  logic. To address this challenge, we introduce <b>AD-MIR</b>, a framework designed
  to decode advertising intent via a two-stage architecture. First, in the <b>Structure-Aware
  Memory Construction</b> phase, the system converts raw video into a structured database
  by integrating semantic retrieval with exact keyword matching. This approach prioritizes
  fine-grained brand details, such as logos and on-screen text, while dynamically
  filtering out irrelevant background noise to isolate key protagonists. Second, the
  <b>Structured Reasoning Agent</b> mimics a marketing expert through an iterative
  inquiry loop, decomposing the narrative to deduce implicit persuasion tactics. Crucially,
  it employs an evidence-based self-correction mechanism that rigorously validates
  these insights against specific video frames, automatically backtracking when visual
  support is lacking. Evaluation on the AdsQA benchmark demonstrates that AD-MIR achieves
  state-of-the-art performance, surpassing the strongest general-purpose agent, DVD,
  by 1.8 and 9.5 percentage points in strict and relaxed accuracy, respectively. These
  results underscore that effective advertising understanding demands explicitly grounding
  abstract marketing strategies in pixel-level evidence. The code is available at
  https://github.com/Little-Fridge/AD-MIR.
