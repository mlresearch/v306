title: 'Learning syntax without semantics: Disentangled tiny language models'
openreview: p7HVrmZwWB
abstract: 'Language models acquire syntax and world knowledge together, entangling
  the two in ways that limit efficiency and controllability. We show that syntax can
  be learned while suppressing semantic plausibility and world-knowledge cues, yielding
  more efficient and controllable models. We train tiny LMs on grammatical nonsense
  — syntactically well-formed text with semantic content ablated via constrained relexicalization
  (SAMBAL). Models trained on this data perform comparably to standard pretraining
  on syntactic benchmarks (BLiMP, SyntaxGym) while scoring at chance on world knowledge
  probes (EWoK). On targeted grammar-plausibility conflict probes, content-neutral
  models prefer grammaticality where standard models prefer plausibility, and their
  representations show more syntactic vs lexical alignment. On efficiency, disentanglement
  yields substantial sample and parameter gains: in low-resource regimes, a 5M-parameter
  model matches a 30M-parameter baseline at the same data budget. On controllability,
  content-neutral models adapt rapidly to a new domain with minimal exposure, suggesting
  the feasibility of modular post-hoc knowledge specialization.'
