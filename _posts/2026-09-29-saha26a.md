abstract: Most existing cultural evaluation frameworks for large language models (LLMs)
  focus on matching model outputs to ground-truth answers, primarily measuring factual
  cultural awareness. This overlooks whether models internalize broader cultural structure
  and pluralism. We introduce a spectral-analysis-based framework that captures large-scale
  macrostructural patterns in models’ cultural knowledge and evaluate eight LLMs across
  nine cultural domains spanning all five of Newmark’s cultural dimensions and 170
  countries. Comparing with human data, we find that instruction-tuned models align
  more closely with human cultural structure than older models, while increased model
  size does not consistently improve performance. Finally, simulation-based experiments
  show that our proposed spectral metric better predicts a model’s ability to serve
  users from unfamiliar cultural backgrounds than existing ones.
