title: 'ConFlux: Multivariate Time Series in Flux, One Unified Forecast in Confluence'
openreview: qugRvYjaFx
abstract: 'Real-world multivariate time series are inherently in flux: different variables
  evolve asynchronously and interact in complex, time-varying ways, yet accurate forecasting
  requires these dispersed signals to converge into a single unified prediction. This
  structural mismatch between dynamic, heterogeneous inputs and a unified forecasting
  objective poses a fundamental challenge for building general-purpose multivariate
  forecasting models, especially in zero-shot and large-scale settings. To this end,
  inspired by the idea that “<em>all rivers run into the sea</em>”, we propose <b>ConFlux</b>,
  a <em>general-purpose foundation model for multivariate time-series forecasting</em>
  by learning to adaptively integrate cross-channel information under a unified forecasting
  objective. Specifically, ConFlux first reorders variables to reduce cross-variable
  entanglement, then aggregates adjacent variables into compact patches that can be
  processed by a Vision Transformer-style architecture. This design shortens the effective
  context, reduces attention complexity, and provides a unified token representation
  for pre-training and downstream tasks. Experiments on 25 public datasets show that
  ConFlux achieves state-of-the-art performance in zero-shot, fine-tuning, and from-scratch
  settings, while offering faster inference and lower memory usage.'
