title: "$\\textttMulti^2$: Hierarchical Multi-Agent Decision-Making with LLM-Based
  Agents in Interactive Environments"

abstract: A central goal of large language model (LLM) research is to build agentic
  systems that can plan, act, and adapt through sustained interaction with dynamic
  environments. While recent LLM-based agents exhibit impressive contextual reasoning,
  their long-horizon decision-making remains fragile, often suffering from $\textit{objective
  drift}$, where goals and plans drift over extended interactions. We introduce $\texttt{Multi}^2$,
  a hierarchical multi-agent decision-making framework that explicitly decomposes
  agent behavior into complementary roles. A high-level agent ($\texttt{System 1}$)
  focuses on context-aware sub-goal generation using supervised fine-tuning (SFT),
  while a low-level agent ($\texttt{System 2}$) executes atomic actions through offline-to-online
  reinforcement learning (RL) in interactive environments. This separation enables
  stable long-horizon control, mitigates objective drift, and allows efficient adaptation.
  Across diverse interactive environments, $\texttt{Multi}^2$ consistently outperforms
  strong agentic baselines, demonstrating improved robustness and coordination in
  multi-turn interaction. Beyond performance, we introduce and release three hierarchical
  benchmark datasets, filling a long-standing gap in training and evaluating hierarchical
  decision-making for LLM-based agents.
