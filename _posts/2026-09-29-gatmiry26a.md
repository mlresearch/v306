title: High-accuracy and dimension-free sampling with diffusions
openreview: YA9jB86LDw
abstract: Diffusion models have shown remarkable empirical success in sampling from
  rich multi-modal distributions. Their inference relies on numerically solving a
  certain differential equation. This differential equation cannot be solved in closed
  form, and its resolution via discretization typically requires many small iterations
  to produce <em>high-quality</em> samples. More precisely, prior works have shown
  that the iteration complexity of discretization methods for diffusion models scales
  polynomially in the ambient dimension and the inverse accuracy $1/\varepsilon$.
  In this work, we propose a new solver for diffusion models relying on a subtle interplay
  between low-degree approximation and the collocation method, and we prove that its
  iteration complexity scales <em>polylogarithmically</em> in $1/\varepsilon$, yielding
  the first "high-accuracy" guarantee for a diffusion-based sampler that only uses
  (approximate) access to the scores of the data distribution. In addition, our bound
  does not depend explicitly on the ambient dimension; more precisely, the dimension
  affects the complexity of our solver only through the <em>effective radius</em>
  of the support of the target distribution.
