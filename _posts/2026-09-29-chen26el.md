title: Discrete Tilt Matching

abstract: Masked diffusion large language models (dLLMs) are a promising alternative
  to autoregressive generation. While reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms have
  been adapted to be compatible with dLLMs for fine-tuning them, their reliance on
  the computation of the marginal likelihood to evaluate policy objectives is intractable.
  To overcome this, we exploit a dynamical relation between the unmasking posterior
  of the base model and that which targets the reward-tilted distribution to derive
  Discrete Tilt Matching (DTM), an algorithm that avoids intractable likelihood evaluation
  entirely. DTM can be phrased as a cross-entropy loss that only requires forward
  evaluation of rewards and whose variance can be adaptively controlled, improving
  training stability. We motivate DTM on maze planning tasks, and show that fine-tuning
  LLaDA-8B-Instruct with DTM achieves higher accuracy at lower compute costs than
  prior RL-based fine-tuning methods across the Sudoku, Countdown, and MATH500 benchmarks.
