title: 'Less Data, Faster Training: repeating smaller datasets speeds up learning
  via sampling biases'
openreview: qcb833SRNd
abstract: This work investigates the "small-vs-large gap", where repeating on <em>fewer
  samples</em> can lead to <em>compute saving</em> during training compared to using
  a larger dataset. This is observed across algorithmic tasks, architectures and optimizers
  and cannot be explained using prior theory. We argue that the speedup comes from
  appropriate layer-wise growth enabled by <em>sampling biases</em>, which is more
  pronounced when the dataset size is smaller. We provide both theoretical analysis
  and empirical evidence from various interventions. Our results suggest that using
  a smaller dataset with more repetitions is not just a fallback strategy under data
  scarcity, but can be proactively leveraged as a favorable inductive biases for optimization,
  particularly in reasoning tasks.
