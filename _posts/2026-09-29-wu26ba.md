title: 'From Conflict to Consensus: Boosting Medical Reasoning via Multi-Round Agentic
  RAG'

abstract: Large Language Models (LLMs) exhibit high reasoning capacity in medical
  question-answering, but their tendency to produce hallucinations and outdated knowledge
  poses critical risks in healthcare fields. While Retrieval-Augmented Generation
  (RAG) mitigates these issues, existing methods rely on noisy token-level signals
  and lack the multi-round refinement required for complex reasoning. In this paper,
  we propose <b>MA-RAG</b> (<b>M</b>ulti-Round <b>A</b>gentic RAG), a framework that
  facilitates test-time scaling for complex medical reasoning by iteratively evolving
  both external evidence and internal reasoning history within an agentic refinement
  loop. At each round, the agent transforms semantic <b>conflict</b> among candidate
  responses into actionable queries to retrieve external evidence, while optimizing
  history reasoning traces to mitigate long-context degradation. MA-RAG extends the
  <em>self-consistency</em> principle by leveraging the lack of consistency as a proactive
  signal for multi-round agentic reasoning and retrieval, and mirrors a <em>boosting</em>
  mechanism that iteratively minimizes the residual error toward a stable, high-fidelity
  medical <b>consensus</b>. Extensive evaluations across 7 medical Q&A benchmarks
  show that MA-RAG consistently surpasses competitive inference-time scaling and RAG
  baselines, delivering <b>substantial +6.8 points</b> on average accuracy over the
  backbone model. Our code is available at https://github.com/NJU-RL/MA-RAG.
