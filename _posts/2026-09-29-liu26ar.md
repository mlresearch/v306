title: 'TGV-KV: Text-Grounded KV Eviction for Vision-Language Models'
openreview: suQ52Hqr9U
abstract: 'Vision-Language Models (VLMs) inherit the auto-regressive generation paradigm
  and cache the keys and values (KV) of all previous tokens to accelerate inference,
  resulting in memory consumption that scales linearly with context length. This issue
  is particularly pronounced in VLMs due to substantial redundancy in the visual modality.
  Although KV cache eviction approaches can effectively reduce inference memory, they
  often incur significant performance degradation in VLMs, as most are designed for
  language models and overlook the inherent gap between text and vision. By systematically
  analyzing the modality gap in VLMs in this work, we argue that the importance of
  visual information should be grounded in textual guidance and accordingly propose
  a <b>T</b>ext-<b>G</b>rounded KV Eviction method for <b>V</b>LMs (<b>TGV-KV</b>).
  TGV-KV comprises three submodules: <em>(1) Text-Vision Budgeting (TVB)</em> assigns
  budget to each layer based on the mutual information interaction. <em>(2) Text-Weighted
  Ranking (TWR)</em> assesses the priority of text and ranks vision importance based
  on weighted text-image attention. <em>(3) Text-Prioritised Retention (TPR)</em>
  policy strategically preserves text KV to avoid acute information loss. We evaluate
  TGV-KV across five models with different sizes and architectures, showing that TGV-KV
  preserves 99.2% full-KV accuracy on the VizWiz-VQA task with LLaVA-NeXT and boosts
  end-to-end throughput by 52.6% with an extreme retention budget of 5%. Code Link.'
