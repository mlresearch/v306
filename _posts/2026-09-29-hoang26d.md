abstract: Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models enable sparse expert activation, meaning
  that only a subset of the model’s parameters is used during each inference. However,
  to translate this sparsity into practical performance, an expert caching mechanism
  is required. Previous works have proposed hardware-centric caching policies, but
  how these various caching policies interact with each other and different hardware
  specification remains poorly understood. To address this gap, we develop <b>SpecMD</b>,
  a standardized framework for benchmarking ad-hoc cache policies on various hardware
  configurations. Using SpecMD, we perform an exhaustive benchmarking of several MoE
  caching strategies, reproducing and extending prior approaches in controlled settings
  with realistic constraints. Our experiments reveal that MoE expert access is not
  consistent with temporal locality assumptions (e.g LRU, LFU). Motivated by this
  observation, we propose <b>Least-Stale</b>, a novel eviction policy that exploits
  MoE’s predictable expert access patterns to reduce collision misses by up to $85\times$
  over LRU. With such gains, we achieve over $88%$ hit rates with up to $34.7%$ Time-to-first-token
  (TTFT) reduction on OLMoE at only $5%$ or $0.6GB$ of VRAM cache capacity.
