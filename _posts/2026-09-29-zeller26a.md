title: 'MentisOculi: Revealing the Limits of Reasoning with Mental Imagery'

abstract: 'Frontier models are transitioning from <em>multimodal large language models</em>
  (MLLMs) that merely ingest visual information to <em>unified multimodal models</em>
  (UMMs) capable of native interleaved generation. This shift has sparked interest
  in using intermediate visualizations as a reasoning aid, akin to human <em>mental
  imagery</em>. Central to this idea is the ability to form, maintain, and manipulate
  visual representations in a goal-oriented manner. To evaluate and probe this capability,
  we develop MentisOculi, a procedural, stratified suite of multi-step reasoning problems
  amenable to visual solution, tuned to challenge frontier models. Evaluating visual
  strategies ranging from latent tokens to explicit generated imagery, we find they
  generally fail to improve performance. Analysis of UMMs specifically exposes a critical
  limitation: While they possess the textual reasoning capacity to solve a task and
  can sometimes generate correct visuals, they suffer from compounding generation
  errors and fail to leverage even ground-truth visualizations. Our findings suggest
  that despite their inherent appeal, <em>visual thoughts do not yet benefit model
  reasoning</em>. MentisOculi establishes the necessary foundation to analyze and
  close this gap across diverse model families.'
