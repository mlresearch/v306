abstract: The quadratic computational complexity of softmax transformers has become
  a bottleneck in long-context scenarios. In contrast, linear attention model families
  provide a promising direction towards a more efficient sequential model. These linear
  attention models compress past $KV$ values into a single hidden state, thereby efficiently
  reducing complexity during both training and inference. However, their expressivity
  remains limited by the size of their hidden state. Previous work proposed interleaving
  softmax and linear attention layers to reduce computational complexity while preserving
  expressivity. Nevertheless, the efficiency of these models remains bottlenecked
  by their softmax attention layers. In this paper, we propose Neural Attention Search
  Linear (NAtS-L), a framework that applies both linear attention and softmax attention
  operations within the same layer on different tokens. NAtS-L automatically determines
  whether a token can be handled by a linear attention model, i.e., tokens that have
  only short-term impact and can be encoded into fixed-size hidden states, or require
  softmax attention, i.e., tokens that contain information related to long-term retrieval
  and need to be preserved for future queries. By searching for optimal Gated DeltaNet
  and softmax attention combinations across tokens, we show that NAtS-L provides a
  strong yet efficient token-level hybrid architecture.
