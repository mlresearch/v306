abstract: 'Reconstructing atomistic crystal structures from a single noisy STEM projection
  is an ill-posed inverse problem: multiple lattices can explain similar contrast,
  and purely feed-forward models cannot verify physical validity. We present <b>AutoMat</b>,
  a failure-aware agentic <em>controller</em> that performs inference-time hypothesis
  search with <em>closed-loop verification</em> to convert Scanning Transmission Electron
  Microscopy (STEM) images into simulation-ready crystal structures and downstream
  properties. AutoMat composes perception and physics modules—pattern-adaptive denoising,
  physics-guided template retrieval <em>as a state-dependent auxiliary branch</em>,
  symmetry-constrained atomic reconstruction, and MLIP-based relaxation/validation—and
  triggers rollback-and-retry when verification fails. For systematic evaluation,
  we introduce <b>STEM2Mat-Bench</b>, a benchmark dataset containing 450+ annotated
  samples. Performance is assessed using lattice root-mean-square deviation (RMSD),
  formation energy mean absolute error (MAE), and structure matching accuracy. Results
  demonstrate that AutoMat outperforms existing approaches including SOTA models,
  specialized domain tools, and closed-source multimodal large models. This work establishes
  a direct pathway from microscopic characterization to atomic-scale modeling, addressing
  a fundamental challenge in materials science.'
