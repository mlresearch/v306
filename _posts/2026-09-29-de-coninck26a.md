abstract: 'The adoption of computer vision to drive industrial efficiency and safety
  creates a persistent tension between operational utility and worker privacy. Current
  privacy measures, such as post-hoc blurring, are fundamentally flawed: they depend
  on the error-prone detection of sensitive attributes yet do not prevent the data
  from being repurposed for unintended inferences. We posit that industrial computer
  vision must shift from ”hiding secrets” to verifiable data minimization. Formalized
  through the Information Bottleneck principle, we advocate for sensing pipelines
  that are optimized to capture only the features necessary for a declared task (e.g.,
  pose estimation), without requiring labels for what is sensitive. This bounds the
  system’s capacity for unauthorized inferences such as re-identification or function
  creep. We outline an implementation path using modular edge processing and trusted
  execution environments, and identify key research challenges for making verifiable
  purpose limitation a prerequisite for responsible industrial AI.'
