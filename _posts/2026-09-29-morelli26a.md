title: 'Partial Fusion of Neural Networks: Efficient Tradeoffs Between Ensembles and
  Weight Aggregation'
openreview: lvRLG6C0zZ
abstract: Ensembles of neural networks typically outperform individual networks but
  incur large computational costs, whereas weight aggregation produces less costly,
  yet also less accurate, aggregate models. We introduce partial fusion of networks,
  which interpolates between ensembles and weight aggregation and thus allows for
  a flexible tradeoff between computational cost and performance. A direct way to
  achieve this is to extend existing weight aggregation methods based on neuron-level
  similarity between different networks, where partial fusion then only aggregates
  weights of neurons which are most similar. We showcase one particular method to
  jointly identify which neurons are most similar and match them via partial optimal
  transport. Further, we consider the more general perspective of weight aggregation
  and partial fusion as generalized pruning of ensemble models, where neurons cannot
  just be deleted, but also linearly combined. Finally, we show that generalized pruning
  applied to a single network yields similar benefits as partial fusion by allowing
  for a tradeoff between isolating, deleting, and linearly combining neurons based
  on similarity.
