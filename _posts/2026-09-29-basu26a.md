title: 'Performative Policy Gradient: Optimality in Performative Reinforcement Learning'
openreview: 9qbzrMIw63
abstract: Post-deployment machine learning algorithms often influence the environments
  they act in, and thus <em>shift</em> the underlying dynamics that the standard reinforcement
  learning (RL) methods ignore. While designing optimal algorithms in this <em>performative</em>
  setting has recently been studied in supervised learning, the RL counterpart remains
  under-explored. In this paper, we prove the performative counterparts of the performance
  difference lemma and the policy gradient theorem in RL, and introduce the <b>Performative
  Policy Gradient</b> algorithm <b>PePG</b>. <b>PePG</b> is the first policy gradient
  algorithm designed to account for performativity in RL. Under softmax parametrisation,
  and also with and without entropy regularisation, we prove that <b>PePG</b> converges
  to <em>performatively optimal policies</em>, i.e. policies that remain optimal under
  the distribution shifts induced by themselves. Thus, <b>PePG</b> significantly extends
  the prior works in Performative RL that achieves <em>performative stability</em>
  but not optimality. Our empirical analysis on standard performative RL environments
  validate that <b>PePG</b> outperforms the existing performative RL algorithms aiming
  for stability.
