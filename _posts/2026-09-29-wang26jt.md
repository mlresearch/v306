abstract: Associative memory has long underpinned the design of sequential models.
  Beyond recall, humans reason by <em>projecting future states and selecting goal-directed
  actions</em>, a capability that modern language models increasingly require but
  do not natively encode. While prior work uses reinforcement learning or test-time
  training, planning remains external to the model architecture. We formulate reasoning
  as <em>optimal control</em> and introduce the <em>Test-Time Control (TTC)</em> layer,
  which performs finite-horizon <em>LQR</em> planning over latent states at inference
  time, represents a <em>value function</em> within neural architectures, and leverages
  it as the nested objective to enable <em>planning before prediction</em>. To ensure
  scalability, we derive a hardware-efficient LQR solver based on a symplectic formulation
  and implement it as a fused CUDA kernel, enabling parallel execution with minimal
  overhead. Integrated as an adapter into pretrained LLMs, TTC layers improve mathematical
  reasoning performance by up to +27.8% on MATH-500 and 2-3$\times$ Pass@8 improvements
  on AMC and AIME, demonstrating that embedding optimal control as an architectural
  component provides an effective and scalable mechanism for reasoning beyond test-time
  training.
