title: Vision Transformer Finetuning Benefits from Non-Smooth Components
openreview: lTXvOhUOl2
abstract: The smoothness of the transformer architecture has been extensively studied
  in the context of generalization, training stability, and adversarial robustness.
  However, its role in transfer learning remains poorly understood. In this paper,
  we analyze the ability of vision transformer components to adapt their outputs to
  changes in inputs, or, in other words, their <em>plasticity</em>. Defined as an
  average rate of change, it captures the sensitivity to input perturbation; in particular,
  a high plasticity implies a low smoothness. Our theoretical analysis and extensive
  experiments – over $1,000$ finetuning runs on large-scale vision transformers –
  showcase that this perspective provides principled guidance in choosing the components
  to prioritize during adaptation. A key takeaway for practitioners is that the high
  plasticity of the attention modules and feedforward layers consistently leads to
  better finetuning performance. Our findings depart from the prevailing assumption
  that smoothness is desirable, offering a novel perspective on transformers’ functional
  properties. The code is available at https://github.com/ambroiseodt/vit-plasticity.
