abstract: Most deep video codecs emphasize low-level motion modeling and remain largely
  semantics-agnostic, which can degrade perceptual quality in complex scenes. We propose
  <b>MoVie</b>, a <b>M</b>ultim<b>o</b>dal <b>Vi</b>d<b>e</b>o compression framework
  built on a Text-guided Video Transformer–CNN Mixed block (<em>Text-VideoTCM</em>).
  MoVie adopts a video-centric architecture that jointly models local spatial structures
  and temporal dynamics via window-based processing, delivering a favorable computation–perception
  trade-off. To incorporate semantics, we introduce dual-stage text fusion with <em>Extractor</em>
  and <em>Injector</em> modules. We further present history-conditioned coding that
  leverages both previous and aggregated historical frames, and a spatial–channel
  factorized entropy model that estimates probabilities over spatial neighborhoods
  and channel groups for adaptive bit allocation. Together, these designs reduce redundancy
  and improve rate control and temporal coherence, yielding reconstructions at low
  bitrates. On UVG and MCL-JCV, MoVie achieves <b>$-$50.23%</b> BD-rate for FID and
  <b>$-$14.64%</b> for LPIPS (VGGNet) relative to HM, while requiring only <b>55.76%</b>
  of DCVC-FM’s per-pixel kMACs. A human perceptual study further confirms consistent
  subjective preference over strong baselines.
