abstract: Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) is a cornerstone of
  post-training for large reasoning models, yet widely used algorithms such as Group
  Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) often exhibit <b>diversity collapse</b>. We
  provide a geometric diagnosis by formalizing GRPO as a dynamical flow on the probability
  simplex. Under a mode-based coarse-graining of rollouts, we show that GRPO induces
  a <b>collision field</b> over correct modes, monotonically pushing towards simplex
  vertices and thus yielding a <b>winner-take-all</b> regime. To address this systematically,
  we introduce <b>G$^2$RPO (Geometric GRPO)</b>, which reshapes RLVR via principled
  <b>vector-field editing</b>. Concretely, we intervene at the advantage level by
  adding granularity bonuses inversely proportional to mode probabilities, encouraging
  underrepresented correct modes. The bonus has a natural geometric interpretation,
  and its potential performance side effects can be mitigated, thereby avoiding the
  usual accuracy–diversity trade-off. In experiments with 7B and 14B models trained
  on a math reasoning task and evaluated on <b>AIME 2024/2025</b>, GRPO loses up to
  <b>57%</b> of active correct modes. In contrast, G$^{2}$RPO increases active correct-mode
  coverage by <b>172%–205%</b>, reduces concentration on any single correct mode,
  prevents the late-stage <em>entropy crash</em>, and improves pass@1 by <b>+1.4</b>
  to <b>+7.9</b> points relative to GRPO. Overall, diversity is not merely a regularizer
  but a <b>geometric property</b> to be controlled to improve the model without trapping
  it in a single dominant strategy.
