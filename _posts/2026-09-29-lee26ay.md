abstract: Training large language models via self-play often suffers from a persistent
  iteration-collapse, where performance initially improves but subsequently regresses
  as training iterations increase. We analyze this phenomenon as arising from cross-iteration
  degeneration, where the task-generation distribution becomes increasingly confined
  to a narrow subset of familiar (seen) problems, weakening the effective learning
  signal and destabilizing training. To address this issue, we propose a plug-in approach
  that augments existing self-play pipelines with a one-class novelty reward. A Seen
  Detector trained on a historical buffer of previously used training problems identifies
  in-support instances and discourages redundant generation by the questioner, thereby
  steering exploration toward under-explored yet learnable regions. Experimental results
  show that the proposed method mitigates iteration-collapse during iterative training
  and yields consistent improvements.
