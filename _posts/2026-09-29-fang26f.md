abstract: As frontier AI systems become increasingly capable, concerns about deceptive
  behaviors have intensified. Unlike hallucinations, which stem from capability limitations,
  deception involves strategically misleading responses despite correct internal representations.
  While prior work has primarily studied deception in text-only settings, little is
  known about how such behaviors manifest in multimodal large language models. In
  this work, we systematically investigate multimodal deception and introduce <em>MM-DeceptionBench</em>,
  the first benchmark designed to evaluate deceptive behaviors in vision–language
  models across six realistic categories. We find that existing text-centric monitoring
  approaches are insufficient in multimodal settings due to the complexity of cross-modal
  reasoning. To address this gap, we propose <em>debate with images</em>, a multi-agent
  evaluation framework that enforces visual grounding through adversarial debate.
  Experiments show that this approach achieves substantially higher agreement with
  human judgments than MLLM-as-a-judge baselines, improving Cohen’s kappa by up to
  1.5$\times$ and accuracy by up to 1.25$\times$ on GPT-4o.
