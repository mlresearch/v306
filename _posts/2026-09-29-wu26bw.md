abstract: 'Safety alignment in large language models (LLMs) and multimodal large language
  models (MLLMs) is commonly assumed to operate as a near-binary threshold mechanism.
  We challenge this assumption by revealing that safety behavior is governed by an
  <em>instability region</em> where small perturbations induce stochastic refusal
  decisions rather than deterministic outcomes. We develop a multi-metric diagnostic
  framework combining external and internal signals to characterize this instability.
  Through systematic experiments, we identify a characteristic <em>diagnostic signature</em>:
  inputs in unstable regimes exhibit elevated output uncertainty yet <em>decreased</em>
  internal safety activation, a decoupling phenomenon that explains why detection-based
  defenses fail against sophisticated attacks. Building on this framework, we introduce
  <b>Furina</b>, a jailbreak attack that deliberately induces this signature through
  fragmented, scene-anchored prompts without model-specific optimization. Furina outperforms
  strong single-turn and multi-turn baselines on HarmBench and achieves competitive
  results on MM-SafetyBench, demonstrating that uncertainty amplification provides
  a principled and transferable mechanism for understanding safety vulnerabilities.
  Code is available at: https://github.com/0xCavaliers/Furina_Jailbreak.'
