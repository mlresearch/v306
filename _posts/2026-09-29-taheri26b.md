title: Probabilistic Robustness Certificates against Adversarial Attacks
openreview: itrFag3Z9v
abstract: The growing use of machine learning in safety-critical settings heightens
  vulnerability to <em>adversarial attacks</em>. Existing defense mechanisms typically
  either lack formal guarantees or depend on restrictive assumptions about the model
  family, the threat model, or the perturbation budget, and many only offer point-wise
  certification. Importantly, they often overlook the inherent stochasticity of modern
  training pipelines, which undermines their practical reliability. We introduce a
  probabilistic framework that views gradient-based training as a <em>discrete-time
  stochastic dynamical system</em> and formulates adversarial robustness as a safety
  verification task. Leveraging <em>barrier certificates</em> (BCs), we derive sufficient
  conditions to probabilistically certify a robust radius against worst-case ${\ell}_p$-bounded
  perturbation, guaranteeing that the final model parameters remain within a safe
  set. For tractable computation, we represent BCs with neural networks and obtain
  <em>probably approximately correct</em> (PAC) guarantees through a <em>scenario
  convex problem</em>. Our method identifies the largest certified radius for which
  the trained model is probabilistically accurate with a specified confidence level.
  Experiments on MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR-10 show that our framework offers formal robustness
  guarantees under stochastic training, while being model-agnostic and not requiring
  prior knowledge of the attack strategy.
