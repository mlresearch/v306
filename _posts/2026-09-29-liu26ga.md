title: 'RedVisor: Reasoning-Aware Prompt Injection Defense via Zero-Copy KV Cache
  Reuse'
openreview: hLLdM61qen
abstract: 'Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly vulnerable to <em>Prompt
  Injection (PI)</em> attacks, where adversarial instructions hidden within retrieved
  contexts hijack the model’s execution flow. Current defenses typically face a critical
  trade-off: <em>prevention-based</em> fine-tuning often degrades general utility
  via the "alignment tax", while <em>detection-based</em> filtering incurs prohibitive
  latency and memory costs. To bridge this gap, we propose <b>RedVisor</b>, a unified
  framework that synthesizes the explainability of detection systems with the seamless
  integration of prevention strategies. To the best of our knowledge, RedVisor is
  the first approach to leverage fine-grained reasoning paths to simultaneously <em>detect</em>
  attacks and <em>guide</em> the model’s safe response. We implement this via a lightweight,
  removable adapter positioned atop the frozen backbone. This adapter serves a dual
  function: it first generates an explainable analysis that precisely localizes the
  injection and articulates the threat, which then explicitly conditions the model
  to reject the malicious command. Uniquely, the adapter is active only during this
  reasoning phase and is effectively muted during the subsequent response generation.
  This architecture yields two distinct advantages: (1) it mathematically preserves
  the backbone’s original utility on benign inputs; and (2) it enables a novel <b>KV
  Cache Reuse</b> strategy, eliminating the redundant prefill computation inherent
  to decoupled pipelines. We further pioneer the integration of this defense into
  the vLLM serving engine with custom kernels. Experiments demonstrate that RedVisor
  outperforms state-of-the-art defenses in detection accuracy and throughput while
  incurring negligible utility loss.'
