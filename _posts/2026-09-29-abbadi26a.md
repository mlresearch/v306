title: What Preferences Can—and Cannot—Predict in Multi-Agent Online Learning
openreview: 5W30WwL8wt
abstract: 'We examine the interplay between ordinal, preference-based solution concepts
  in games and the long-run behavior of game dynamics, asking in particular to what
  extent the combinatorial data of a game—its <em>preference graph</em>—determine
  the outcomes of no-regret learning dynamics—such as <em>follow-the-regularized-leader</em>
  (FTRL). In one direction, we show that the skeleton of every <em>dynamically stable</em>
  set (i.e. the set of pure profiles it contains) must also be <em>preferentially
  stable</em>, that is, it must be closed under profitable deviations. We then ask
  the converse question: <em>when do preferences determine the long-run behavior of
  the players’ learning dynamics?</em> We begin by showing that preferences characterize
  asymptotic stability in the case of <em>subgames</em>—i.e. subsets of pure profiles
  obtained by restricting players’ action sets. Beyond this case however, the equivalence
  between dynamic and preferential stability collapses: concretely, we construct a
  three-player game with a preferentially stable set whose span is dynamically <em>unstable</em>,
  showing in this way that preferences <em>do not suffice</em> as a criterion of dynamic
  stability. We then bridge this gap via the notion of <em>resilience under aggregate
  deviations</em>, an easy-to-check payoff-based condition that guarantees asymptotic
  stability of arbitrary spans of pure strategies.'
