title: 'SparseOpt: Addressing Normalization-induced Gradient Skew in Sparse Training'

abstract: Dynamic Sparse Training (DST) methods train neural networks by maintaining
  sparsity while dynamically adapting the network topology. Despite the promise of
  reduced computation, DST methods converge significantly slower than dense training,
  often requiring comparable training time to achieve similar accuracy. We demonstrate
  both analytically and empirically that Batch Normalization (BN) adversely affects
  sparse training, and propose SparseOpt — a sparsity-aware optimizer — to address
  this. Experiments on ResNet models across CIFAR-100 and ImageNet demonstrate consistently
  faster convergence and improved generalization with our proposed method. Our work
  highlights the limitations of current normalization layers in sparse training and
  provides the first systematic study of the interaction between Batch Normalization,
  sparse layers, and DST, taking a significant step toward making DST practically
  competitive with dense training.
