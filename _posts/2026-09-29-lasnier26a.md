title: 'Translation Heads: Disentangling meaning from language in LLM-based machine
  translation'
openreview: JKSDWukEgN
abstract: 'Mechanistic Interpretability (MI) seeks to explain how neural networks
  implement their capabilities, but the scale of Large Language Models (LLMs) has
  limited prior MI work in Machine Translation (MT) to word-level analyses. We study
  sentence-level MT from a mechanistic perspective by analyzing attention heads to
  understand how LLMs internally encode and distribute translation functions. We decompose
  MT into two subtasks: producing text in the target language (i.e. target language
  identification) and preserving the input sentence’s meaning (i.e. sentence equivalence).
  Across three families of open-source models and 20 translation directions, we find
  that distinct, sparse sets of attention heads specialize in each subtask. Based
  on this insight, we construct subtask-specific steering vectors and show that modifying
  just 1% of the relevant heads enables instruction-free MT performance comparable
  to instruction-based prompting, while ablating these heads selectively disrupts
  their corresponding translation functions.'
