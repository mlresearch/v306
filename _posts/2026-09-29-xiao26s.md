abstract: 'AI Scientists have shown promising progress across multiple stages of the
  research pipeline, among which automatic scientific paper writing remains a formidable
  challenge. The Introduction writing is especially challenging, which demands not
  only linguistic fluency, but logical soundness and verifiable faithfulness. Most
  AI-assisted methods treat the task as text generation instead of reasoning and structuring,
  leading to severe drawbacks, <em>e.g.</em>, hallucinating citations. To address
  this, we first formulate the Content-Conditional Introduction Generation (CCIG)
  task, which requires grounding the Introduction in the paper’s core evidence. We
  then propose <b>LECTOR</b>, a novel <b>Logic-Expression Co-Reinforcement Learning</b>
  framework that can strictly follow the scientist’s logic, add high-quality citations
  and keep structured expressions. LECTOR first constructs a logic-reasoning graph
  from the paper’s main body to serve as a verifiable logical blueprint. Subsequently,
  it employs a Logic-Expression Co-Rewarding mechanism to jointly optimize for both
  the graph’s structural fidelity and the final narrative’s quality. We conduct a
  dataset from <em>Nature Communications</em> papers to assess our method. Extensive
  experiments show consistent improvements in both logic fidelity and Introduction
  generation quality metrics, <em>e.g.</em>, Graph Quality <b>(+26.7%)</b>, Citation
  Quality <b>(+8.6%)</b>, and Paper Consistency <b>(+3.3%)</b>. Code and data are
  available at: https://github.com/Xiao-Youth/LECTOR'
