title: 'A Positive Case for Faithfulness: LLM Self-Explanations Help Predict Model

abstract: 'LLM self-explanations are often presented as a promising tool for AI oversight,
  yet their faithfulness to the model’s true reasoning process is poorly understood.
  Existing faithfulness metrics have critical limitations, typically relying on identifying
  unfaithfulness via adversarial prompting or detecting reasoning errors. These methods
  overlook the predictive value of explanations. We introduce <em>Normalized Simulatability
  Gain</em> (NSG), a general and scalable metric based on the idea that a faithful
  explanation should allow an observer to learn a model’s decision-making criteria,
  and thus better predict its behavior on related inputs. We evaluate 18 frontier
  proprietary and open-weight models, e.g., Gemini 3, GPT-5.2, and Claude 4.5, on
  7,000 counterfactuals from popular datasets covering health, business, and ethics.
  We find self-explanations substantially improve prediction of model behavior (11-37%
  NSG). Self-explanations also provide more predictive information than explanations
  generated by external models, even when those models are stronger. This implies
  an advantage from self-knowledge that external explanation methods cannot replicate.
  Our approach also reveals that, across models, 5-15% of self-explanations are egregiously
  misleading. Despite their imperfections, we show a positive case for self-explanations:
  they encode information that helps predict model behavior.'
