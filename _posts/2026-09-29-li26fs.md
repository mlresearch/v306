abstract: Bimanual dexterous manipulation relies on integrating multimodal inputs
  to perform complex real-world tasks. To address the challenges of effectively combining
  these modalities, we propose DECO, a decoupled multimodal diffusion transformer
  that disentangles vision, proprioception, and tactile signals through specialized
  conditioning pathways, enabling structured and controllable integration of multimodal
  inputs, with a lightweight adapter for parameter-efficient injection of additional
  signals. Alongside DECO, we release DECO-50 dataset for bimanual dexterous manipulation
  with tactile sensing, consisting of 50 hours of data and over 5M frames, collected
  via teleoperation on real dual-arm robots. We train DECO on DECO-50 and conduct
  extensive real-world evaluation with over 2,000 robot rollouts. Experimental results
  show that DECO achieves the best performance across all tasks, with a 72.25% average
  success rate and a 21% improvement over the baseline. Moreover, the tactile adapter
  brings an additional 10.25% average success rate across all tasks and a 20% gain
  on complex contact-rich tasks while tuning less than 10% of the model parameters.
