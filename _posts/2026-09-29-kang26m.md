title: Instance-Level Costs for Nuanced Classifier Evaluation
openreview: qMI1xD8O3x
abstract: Standard classification treats all errors equally, but in applications such
  as content moderation and medical screening, mistakes on clear-cut cases are more
  costly than errors on ambiguous ones. From a contextual bandit framework, we propose
  normalized excess cost (NEC), a metric that weighs classification errors by per-example
  costs and reduces to standard error rate when costs are uniform. Costs can derive
  from annotator vote margins, distance from decision thresholds, or confidence ratings.
  Across text, image, and tabular benchmarks, we find that NEC is often substantially
  lower than error rate—models with 5% error rate can achieve 1.8% NEC—revealing that
  most mistakes concentrate on ambiguous, low-cost examples. We also find that the
  evaluated methods—including cost-aware training, focal loss, and post-hoc thresholding—do
  not consistently improve NEC across datasets. Our framework provides a practical
  methodology for deriving and evaluating instance-level misclassification costs.
  Code and reproducibility artifacts are available at https://github.com/KabirKang/instance-level-costs-paper-icml.
