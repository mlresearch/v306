abstract: The agency expected of Agentic Large Language Models goes beyond answering
  correctly, requiring autonomy to set goals and decide what to explore. We term this
  <em>investigatory intelligence</em>, distinguishing it from <em>executional intelligence</em>,
  which merely completes assigned tasks. Data Science provides a natural testbed,
  as real-world analysis starts from raw data rather than explicit queries, yet few
  benchmarks focus on it. To address this, we introduce <b>Deep Data Research (DDR)</b>,
  an open-ended task where LLMs autonomously extract key insights from databases,
  and <b>DDR-Bench</b>, a large-scale, checklist-based benchmark that enables verifiable
  evaluation. Results show that while frontier models display emerging agency, long-horizon
  exploration remains challenging. Our analysis highlights that effective investigatory
  intelligence depends not only on agent scaffolding or merely scaling, but also on
  intrinsic strategies of agentic models.
