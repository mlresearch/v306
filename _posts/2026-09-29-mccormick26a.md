abstract: Researchers rarely observe the mechanistic drivers of scientific processes
  directly. Instead, they observe proxies, which are measurable quantities correlated
  with one or more of these latent drivers. This position paper argues that mechanistic
  learning from high-dimensional proxies is generically underdetermined. Many incompatible
  mechanisms induce essentially the same observational relationships on the support
  of the data, so predictive success and coherent explanations are insufficient evidence
  of mechanism discovery. Machine Learning (ML) and Artificial Intelligence (AI) models
  can therefore generate scientific hypotheses that sound plausible while the underlying
  mechanism remains unidentified. The problem is particularly acute with large language
  models (LLMs), which tend to collapse entire equivalence classes of explanations
  into a single fluent narrative. The paper proposes concrete standards for “mechanistic
  AI,” norms that are necessary if AI-centered workflows are to support science rather
  than merely simulate it.
