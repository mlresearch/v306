title: 'R-Diverse: Mitigating Diversity Illusion in Self-Play LLM Training'
openreview: DZiuKVvrJW
abstract: 'Self-play bootstraps LLM reasoning through an iterative Challenger–Solver
  loop: the Challenger is trained to generate questions that target the Solver’s capabilities,
  and the Solver is optimized on the generated data to expand its reasoning skills.
  However, existing frameworks like R-Zero often exhibit non-sustained improvement,
  where early gains degrade as self-play continues. We identify a key failure mode,
  <b>Diversity Illusion</b>, where the Solver’s training signals appear diverse yet
  collapse into recurring underlying patterns. It manifests as (1) <em>Local Diversity
  Illusion</em>, where diversity is enforced only within-batch, inducing cross-iteration
  mode cycling; and (2) <em>Surface Diversity Illusion</em>, where questions vary
  superficially but require near-identical reasoning skills. To mitigate them, we
  propose R-Diverse with two aligned innovations: <b>Memory-Augmented Penalty (MAP)</b>,
  which uses a persistent memory bank to discourage recycling across iterations, and
  <b>Skill-Aware Measurement (SAM)</b>, which evaluates diversity by the reasoning
  skills exercised rather than surface variation of questions. Across 10 math and
  general reasoning benchmarks, R-Diverse sustains gains over more iterations and
  consistently outperforms prior self-play methods.'
