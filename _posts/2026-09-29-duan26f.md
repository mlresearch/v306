abstract: 'Though biological foundation models (Bio-FMs) have delivered strong performance
  across biomedical tasks, their robustness to small-but-real perturbations is underexplored.
  In this work, we ask: Are Bio-FMs robust for real-world use? What perturbations
  compromise their reliability? Our pilot study suggests that due to subtle biological
  data curation issues and common machine-learning (ML) processing choices, Bio-FMs
  suffer from two complementary perturbation sources: biologically plausible perturbations
  (capturing experimental corruptions and curation artifacts) and ML-induced transformations
  (capturing preprocessing, data augmentation, and embedding choices). Guided by this
  taxonomy, we design perturbation suites that mimic corruptions frequently encountered
  in biological experiments, and we systematically probe how transformations in the
  ML pipeline reshape model behavior. By conducting 2,128 experiments over 11 state-of-the-art
  Bio-FMs on 7 bio-tasks, we show that most Bio-FMs are vulnerable to both biological
  perturbations and ML transformations, revealing underappreciated robustness gaps
  that can directly translate into deployment risk. Interestingly, we find that subtle
  biological perturbations, which are often imperceptible to current measurement tools,
  can induce severe discrepancies in Bio-FM outputs and lead to critical failures,
  yet cryo-EM models (e.g., CryoDRGN) exhibit a surprising level of robustness even
  under worst-case perturbations. Our study for the first time surfaces critical failure
  modes and provides a principled perspective for evaluating the robustness of Bio-FMs.'
