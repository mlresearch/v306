title: When Does Adaptation Win? Scaling Laws for Meta-Learning in Quantum Control
openreview: Gu9pw3D6vT
abstract: Quantum hardware suffers from intrinsic device heterogeneity and environmental
  drift, forcing practitioners to choose between suboptimal non-adaptive controllers
  or costly per-device recalibration. We derive a scaling law lower bound for meta-learning
  showing that the adaptation gain (expected fidelity improvement from task-specific
  gradient steps) saturates exponentially with gradient steps and scales linearly
  with task variance, providing a quantitative criterion for when adaptation justifies
  its overhead. Validation on quantum gate calibration shows negligible benefits for
  low-variance tasks but $>$ 40% fidelity gains on two-qubit gates under extreme out-of-distribution
  conditions (10$\times$ the training noise), with implications for reducing per-device
  calibration time on cloud quantum processors. Further validation on classical linear-quadratic
  control confirms these laws emerge from general optimization geometry rather than
  quantum-specific physics. We further introduce a few-shot pre-adaptation protocol
  that estimates the optimal adaptation budget from $N =3–5$ probe steps within 3–19%
  relative error across out-of-distribution regimes.
