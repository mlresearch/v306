title: Characterization of Gaussian Universality Breakdown in High-Dimensional Empirical
  Risk Minimization
openreview: UHQDfvZBFi
abstract: We study high-dimensional convex empirical risk minimization (ERM) under
  general non-Gaussian data designs. By heuristically extending the Convex Gaussian
  Min–Max Theorem (CGMT) to non-Gaussian settings, we derive an asymptotic min–max
  characterization of key statistics, enabling approximation of the mean $\mu_{\hat{\theta}}$
  and covariance $C_{\hat{\theta}}$ of the ERM estimator $\hat{\theta}$. Specifically,
  under a concentration assumption on the data matrix and standard regularity conditions
  on the loss and regularizer, we show that for a test covariate $x$ independent of
  the training data, the projection $\hat{\theta}^\top x$ approximately follows the
  convolution of the (generally non-Gaussian) distribution of $\mu_{\hat{\theta}}^\top
  x$ with an independent centered Gaussian variable of variance $\mathrm{tr}\!\big(C_{\hat{\theta}}\,\mathbb{E}[xx^\top]\big)$.
  This result clarifies the scope and limits of Gaussian universality for ERMs. Numerical
  simulations across diverse losses and models are provided to validate our theoretical
  predictions and qualitative insights.
