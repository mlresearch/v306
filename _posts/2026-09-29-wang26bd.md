title: 'HE-SNR: Uncovering Latent Logic via Entropy for Guiding Mid-Training on SWE-bench'
openreview: FoK74p8a2Z
abstract: <b>SWE-bench</b> has emerged as the premier benchmark for evaluating Large
  Language Models on complex software engineering tasks. While these capabilities
  are fundamentally acquired during the <em>mid-training</em> phase and subsequently
  elicited during Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), there remains a critical deficit in
  metrics capable of guiding mid-training effectively. Standard metrics such as Perplexity
  (PPL) are compromised by the "Long-Context Tax" and exhibit weak correlation with
  downstream SWE performance. In this paper, we bridge this gap by first introducing
  a rigorous data filtering strategy. Crucially, we propose the <b>Entropy Compression
  Hypothesis</b>, redefining intelligence not by scalar Top-1 compression, but by
  the capacity to structure uncertainty into Entropy-Compressed States of low orders
  ("reasonable hesitation"). Grounded in this fine-grained entropy analysis, we formulate
  a novel metric, <b>HE-SNR</b> (High-Entropy Signal-to-Noise Ratio). We validate
  our approach on models with up to 560B parameters across different context windows
  (32K/128K). This work provides both the theoretical foundation and practical tools
  for optimizing the latent potential of LLMs in complex engineering domains.
