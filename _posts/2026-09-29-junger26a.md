abstract: Diffusion language models generate text by iteratively denoising an entire
  response in parallel, holding a noisy draft of the answer at every step. We show
  that these drafts surface salient entities early in the denoising trajectory, making
  them a useful lookahead signal for retrieval-augmented generation. We further show
  that grounding generation in retrieved evidence sharply reduces inter-token dependence,
  making RAG particularly well suited to parallel decoding. We exploit both through
  Self-Augmenting Retrieval for Diffusion Language Models (SARDI), a dynamic RAG framework
  that uses these intermediate drafts to guide retrieval during denoising. SARDI is
  training-free, retriever-agnostic, and applicable to any reasoning-capable discrete
  diffusion language model. Across five multi-hop QA benchmarks, SARDI outperforms
  training-free diffusion and autoregressive retrieval baselines at up to $5\times$
  higher throughput.
