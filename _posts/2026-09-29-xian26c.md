abstract: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have shown strong performance in
  multi-image cross-modal retrieval, yet suffer from severe position bias, where predictions
  are dominated by input order rather than semantic relevance. Through empirical analysis,
  we identify a phenomenon termed Logit-Attention Divergence, in which output logits
  are heavily biased while internal attention maps remain well-aligned with relevant
  visual evidence. This observation reveals a fundamental limitation of existing logit-level
  calibration methods such as PriDe. Based on this insight, we propose a training-free,
  attention-guided debiasing framework that leverages intrinsic attention signals
  for instance-level correction at inference time, requiring only a minimal calibration
  set with negligible computational overhead. Experiments on MS-COCO-based benchmarks
  show that our method substantially improves permutation invariance and achieves
  state-of-the-art performance, enhancing accuracy by over 40% compared to baselines.
  Code is available at https://github.com/brightXian/LAD.
