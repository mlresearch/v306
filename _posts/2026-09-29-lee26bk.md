abstract: 'A surge of recent advancements has consistently highlighted the superiority
  of multimodal learning over unimodal approaches across a variety of tasks. However,
  the theoretical foundations elucidating this advantage remain underexplored: existing
  theoretical analyses are often constrained by tight assumptions, and lack empirical
  validation. In this paper, we link this gap by proposing a novel theoretical framework
  grounded in <em>convolutional smoothing</em>, offering a new perspective on how
  multimodal learning contributes to a smoother loss landscape compared to unimodal
  learning. Building upon this theoretical foundation, we introduce a simple yet effective
  distributional training approach based on stochastic modality pairing instead of
  fixed pairing; thus, further promoting flatter landscape via convolutional smoothing.
  Our empirical results across various multimodal datasets demonstrate that multimodal
  models not only achieve better performance but also exhibit smoother loss landscape,
  which represent better robustness and generalization.'
