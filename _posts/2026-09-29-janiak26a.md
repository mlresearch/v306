abstract: 'Out-of-distribution (OOD) detection is critical for reliable deployment
  of vision models, and Mahalanobis-based detectors remain strong baselines. However,
  their performance varies widely across modern pretrained representations, making
  it unclear which feature-space properties determine success or failure. Across diverse
  foundation-model backbones and Mahalanobis variants, we show that performance is
  highly representation-dependent and can shift substantially with pretraining data
  and fine-tuning. We link this variability to in-distribution geometry and identify
  a two-term ID summary that consistently tracks Mahalanobis OOD behavior across detectors:
  within-class spectral structure and local intrinsic dimensionality. Finally, we
  introduce radially scaled $\ell_2$ normalization, $\phi_\beta(z)=z/\|z\|^\beta$,
  a direction-preserving transformation that changes feature radii and exposes a different
  ID geometry to the same quadratic detector. Selecting $\beta$ from ID-only geometry
  signals generally outperforms fixed normalization baselines.'
