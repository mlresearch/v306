abstract: 'Treating the outcome of machine learning as a stable, identifiable artifact
  is implicit in language, tooling, and governance. This position paper examines whether
  a trained system admits context-appropriate criteria of identity. I show that neither
  functional behavior nor internal structure suffices: behavioral equivalence is underdetermined
  by finite data, while modern architectures admit multiple, structurally distinct
  realizations of the same function. Consequently, practices that treat learned systems
  as stable objects presuppose equivalence relations that are rarely made explicit.
  I do not propose abandoning such practices. Instead, I articulate the reasonably
  minimal conditions under which identity claims grounded in behavior, structure,
  or training process can be meaningfully interpreted, with implications for reproducibility,
  traceability, and governance.'
