abstract: 'Recent benchmarks reveal that single-cell perturbation response models
  are often outperformed by simply predicting the dataset mean. Through large-scale
  <em>in silico</em> simulations, together with analyses of two real-world perturbation
  datasets, we trace this anomaly to a metric artifact: unweighted error metrics systematically
  reward mean predictions when perturbation effects are sparse. To address this limitation,
  we introduce differentially expressed gene (DEG)-aware metrics—weighted mean-squared
  error (WMSE) and weighted delta $R^{2}$ ($R^{2}_{w}(\Delta)$)—that sensitively measure
  error in niche, perturbation-specific signals. We further propose explicit negative
  and positive performance baselines to calibrate these metrics. Under this framework,
  the mean baseline sinks to null performance, while genuinely informative predictors
  are correctly rewarded. Finally, we show that using WMSE as a training objective
  reduces mode collapse and improves predictive performance across multiple model
  architectures.'
