abstract: Existing voice deepfake detection and localization models rely heavily on
  representations extracted from speech foundation models (SFMs). However, downstream
  finetuning has now reached a state of diminishing returns. In this paper, we shift
  the focus to pretraining and propose a novel recipe that combines <em>bottleneck
  masked embedding prediction with flow-matching based spectrogram reconstruction</em>.
  The outcome, <em>Alethia</em>, is the first foundational audio encoder for various
  voice deepfake detection and localization tasks. We evaluate on 5 different tasks
  with 56 benchmark datasets, and note <em>Alethia</em> significantly outperforms
  state-of-the-art SFMs with superior robustness to real-world perturbations and zero-shot
  generalization to unseen domains (e.g., singing deepfakes). We also demonstrate
  the limitation of discrete targets in masked token prediction, and show the importance
  of <em>continuous embedding</em> prediction and <em>generative pretraining</em>
  for capturing deepfake artifacts.
