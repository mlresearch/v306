title: 'BioFormer: Rethinking Cross-Subject Generalization via Spectral Structural
  Alignment in Biomedical Time-Series'
openreview: 6I7yMzQBts
abstract: Cross-subject generalization in biomedical time-series aims to learn representations
  that generalize to unseen subjects while suppressing subject-specific variability.
  Most existing methods implicitly suppress the variability through model building
  or subject adversarial learning, but rarely model it explicitly. We introduce <b>spectral
  drift</b> as a new perspective to characterize subject specific variability. Specifically,
  BTS signals under the same label often share consistent oscillatory structure, yet
  exhibit subject-dependent magnitude or phase shifts in specific frequency components,
  which we interpret as subject-specific variability. Building on this insight, we
  propose <em>BioFormer</em>. At its core is a Frequency-Band Alignment Module (FBAM)
  that generates band-wise modulation factors from the spectral distribution and adaptively
  adjusts amplitude and phase to align spectral structure, thereby mitigating variability.
  We further pair FBAM with Sample Conditional Layer Normalization, which infers normalization
  parameters from intrinsic signal statistics rather than subject identity, stabilizing
  cross-subject representations. Extensive experiments on six datasets demonstrate
  that BioFormer outperforms 12 baselines, yielding absolute F1-score improvements
  of 6%.
