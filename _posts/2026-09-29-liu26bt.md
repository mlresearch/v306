abstract: Neural scaling laws relate loss to model size in large language models (LLMs),
  yet depth and width may contribute to performance differently, requiring more detailed
  studies. Here, we quantify how depth affects loss via analysis of LLMs and toy residual
  networks. We find loss scales inversely proportional to depth in LLMs, probably
  due to functionally similar layers reducing error through ensemble averaging rather
  than compositional learning or discretizing smooth dynamics. This regime is inefficient
  yet robust and may arise from the architectural bias of residual networks and target
  functions incompatible with smooth dynamics. The findings suggest that improving
  LLM efficiency may require architectural innovations to encourage compositional
  use of depth.
