abstract: 'This position paper argues that reliable AI requires infrastructure for
  human validation of implicit knowledge. AI learns from both explicit knowledge (papers,
  documentation, structured databases) and implicit knowledge (reasoning patterns,
  debugging processes, intermediate steps). Implicit knowledge remains unexternalized
  because documentation cost exceeds perceived value—yet AI learns from it indiscriminately,
  acquiring both beneficial patterns and harmful biases. Current reliability methods
  can only verify explicit knowledge against sources, creating a fundamental gap:
  the most valuable AI capabilities (reasoning, judgment, intuition) are precisely
  those we cannot verify. We propose Knowledge Objects (KOs)—structured artifacts
  that externalize implicit knowledge into forms humans can inspect, verify, and endorse.
  KOs transform verification economics: what was previously too costly to verify becomes
  feasible, enabling accumulated human validation to improve reliability over time.'
